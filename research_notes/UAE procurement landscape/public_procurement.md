# UAE Public Procurement Landscape (as of 19 November 2026)

Scope note: This is federal + emirate-level public procurement, with emphasis on rules, platforms, approval gates and cycle times relevant to an AI procurement assistant for UAE SMEs. Primary sources are preferred; secondary/aggregator claims are explicitly labelled. "Older" labels mark pre-2023/2024 law that may still apply transitively.

## Key Question 1: What is the current federal procurement legal framework, its effective date, and which entities does it cover?

### Takeaway
The current framework is **Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2023 on Procurement in the Federal Government** (issued 27 Nov 2023, in force 1 Dec 2023), now operationalised by **Cabinet Resolution No. 122 of 2024** (issued 4 Nov 2024, published in Official Gazette No. 787 on 14 Nov 2024, **effective 14 May 2025**). It covers federal ministries, central agencies and non-profit independent federal agencies, with a long list of exemptions (including armed forces, construction projects, medicines, PPPs, and government-to-government contracting); pre-existing Cabinet Decision No. 4 of 2019 survives only where not inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- Federal Decree-Law No. 11/2023 was issued 27 November 2023 (Jumada I 13, 1445 H) and, per Art. 45, enters into force the day after Official Gazette publication; MoF confirms the **effective date was 1 December 2023**. It has 45 articles. — [LEXAI mirror of official law text](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [MoF announcement](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no-11-of-2023-regarding-federal-government-procurement/)
- The law was publicly announced by MoF/WAM on 10 January 2024; its objectives include digital procurement, integrity/transparency/competition, public value, sustainable development, SME support and procurement-planning efficiency. — [MoF announcement](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no-11-of-2023-regarding-federal-government-procurement/); [WAM](https://www.wam.ae/en/article/b12y33a-ministry-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no)
- **Scope (Art. 3):** (1) ministries and central agencies exercising federal executive, legislative or judicial powers under Federal Law No. 1/1972; (2) independent federal agencies with independent juridical personality, own budget, special administrative structure and non-profit status. MoF's summary adds "subsidiaries under them". — [LEXAI law text, Art. 3](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [MoF announcement](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no-11-of-2023-regarding-federal-government-procurement/)
- **Exemptions (Art. 4):** armed forces/Ministry of Defence; State Security Service; procurement under international agreements/organisations; **construction projects and contracts**; military/security procurement by Ministry of Interior or security agencies (with ministerial/head approval); state missions abroad; procurement made outside the State for use abroad; establishing/furnishing government offices abroad; **medicines and medical supplies**; PPP/partnership contracts; land and existing-building procurement/lease and asset disposal; investment activities for commercial return; financing and guarantee operations; and any agency/_procurement exempted by Cabinet Resolution after consulting the Ministry. Exempt agencies are still expected to be guided by the law's objectives and best public value. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 4](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [MoF announcement](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no-11-of-2023-regarding-federal-government-procurement/)
- Art. 40: federal agencies and wholly government-owned companies (local or federal) may contract directly with each other without substantive or procedural subjection to the law, provided they perform the work themselves; they may act on each other's behalf. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 40](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165)
- **Executive Regulation:** Cabinet Resolution No. (122) of 2024 on the Executive Regulation of Federal Law No. 11/2023 — issued 4 Nov 2024, Official Gazette 14 Nov 2024 (No. 787), effective **14 May 2025**, status Active, 55 articles. — [Official UAE Legislation text (mirror)](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html); [UAE Legislation listing](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/2709)
- **Older/transitional rules still relevant:** Art. 43 of the law keeps **Cabinet Resolution No. 1/1/2022** (federal digital procurement policy) and **Cabinet Decision No. 4 of 2019** (federal procurement and warehouse management regulation) in force only where they do not conflict with the new law, until implementing regulations/directives/policies are issued. Contracts concluded before 1 Dec 2023 remain governed by their original terms; amendments/renewals/extensions must follow the new law. — [LEXAI law text, Arts. 43–44](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [MoF announcement](https://mof.gov.ae/en/news/ministry-of-finance-announces-federal-decree-law-no-11-of-2023-regarding-federal-government-procurement/)
- Governance split: the **Ministry of Finance** holds the Central Procurement Unit (manages the procurement system, policies, procedures, documentation); each federal agency has a Procurement Organizational Unit, a Requesting Organizational Unit, a Procurement Committee and a Grievance Committee; decisions flow through a **Powers Delegation Matrix** issued by the MoF Minister, which each agency maps to named employees and may request amendments to. — [LEXAI law text, Arts. 5–7](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation, Arts. 1–6](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- Independent federal agencies may adopt their own procurement regulations/policies and their own powers matrix, guided by the federal regulation. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 42.3](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [LEXAI law text, Art. 6.4](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165)
- **Operational manual:** the MoF-published *Federal Government Procurement Procedures Guide* (Arabic only, ~10 MB PDF) is binding on all federal entities and suppliers; its listed principles include sustainability/circular economy, "Zero Government Bureaucracy", localisation/national content, digital technologies and AI. Most numeric thresholds (e.g. the value above which public tender is mandatory) sit in this Manual rather than in the law or executive regulation. — [MoF Procedures Guide page](https://mof.gov.ae/en/public-finance/government-procurement/federal-government-procurement-procedures-guide/); [Executive Regulation, Art. 18.2](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)

### Inferences
- As of 2026 the "newer federal procurement law" is fully in force *and* backed by a detailed executive regulation that has been effective since May 2025; a supplier-facing assistant should treat the 2023 law + 2024 regulation + Arabic Procedures Manual as the current rule-set, with Cabinet Decision 4/2019 only as legacy fallback.
- The long exemption list plus the government-to-government carve-out means a large share of UAE public spending (defence, construction, healthcare supplies, PPPs, semigovernment/ sovereign buyers) sits *outside* the federal regime — buyer type must be identified before applying any rule.
- The Powers Delegation Matrix and mandatory committee/unit roles are the real internal approval gates: buyer "slowness" is often delegation/approval-driven, not supplier-driven.

### Gaps
- Exact monetary thresholds (public-tender threshold, negotiation/reverse-auction threshold, low-value threshold, ICV score weighting by value band) are in the Arabic-only Procedures Manual and were not retrievable in English — I found no reliable published English figures. One low-trust aggregator claims "all federal tenders above AED 500,000 use Etimad"; this conflicts with primary sources and should not be used. — [Low-trust claim](https://insights.epiidosisglobalfin.com/knowledge/uae-government-procurement-tender-guide)
- Whether the UAE is party to the WTO Government Procurement Agreement or GCC procurement commitments could not be confirmed from a primary source in this research.
- The specific Cabinet Resolution number setting ICV requirements (referenced generically in Executive Regulation Art. 14) was not identified.

## Key Question 2: Which official e-tendering platforms are used (federal and emirate level), and what stages do suppliers and buyers move through?

### Takeaway
The federal system is the **Digital Procurement Platform (DPP)** plus the **Federal Supplier Register**, both run by the Ministry of Finance. Abu Dhabi uses the **Abu Dhabi Government Procurement Gate (ADGPG)** under the Department of Government Enablement; Dubai uses **eSupply** (~40 entities), **iSupplier** (Digital Dubai) and entity-specific portals (DEWA, Dubai Police). There is **no single unified UAE tender portal** — registration and tender discovery are fragmented by emirate and by entity.

### Cited Findings
- **"Etimad" is not the UAE platform.** Etimad is the Saudi Ministry of Finance's electronic government-procurement platform (launched 2018) covering tenders, contracts, guarantees and payments. UAE federal procurement runs on MoF's DPP. — [Saudi NCGR](https://www.ncgr.gov.sa/en/etimad-platform); [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/etimad-platform); [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- MoF's DPP "enables full electronic management of procurement activities" and lets suppliers register, participate in tenders, negotiate contracts, acknowledge purchase orders, raise invoices and track order status in real time via web and mobile, 100% electronically. — [MoF Government Procurement hub](https://mof.gov.ae/en/public-finance/government-procurement/); [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- As displayed on MoF's service page (accessed 19 Nov 2026): **76 active business opportunities, 5,000+ active suppliers, 30+ active federal government entities**. The **DPP Catalogue** offers **100,000+ products and services** to government entities. — [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/); [MoF Government Procurement hub](https://mof.gov.ae/en/public-finance/government-procurement/)
- **Federal Supplier Register — supplier types:** Domestic Supplier (in UAE, not free zone/abroad), Free Zone Supplier, Foreign Supplier (no UAE branch), Family/SANAA (Emirati productive families and people of determination), SME (members of the National Programme for SMEs at Ministry of Economy), Freelancer. Registration uses **UAE PASS**; documents: trade licence, VAT registration certificate, owner passport copy; foreign suppliers need tax registration certificate and establishment certificate attested by UAE Embassy in country of origin. **Service completion: 30 working days; no fees; 24x7.** — [MoF supplier registration service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- Registration steps: access MoF website/app → "Start Service" → log in with UAE PASS → accept terms → complete mandatory fields by supplier type → upload documents → submit. Account activates only after review/approval as a qualified supplier. — [MoF supplier registration service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/); [MoF DPP registration manual (PDF)](https://www.mohesr.gov.ae/Ar/AboutTheMinistry/Documents/MoF_DPP_User Manual_Supplier_Registration_ENG_20220420_v8.0.pdf) *(older 2022 manual)*
- **Abu Dhabi:** ADGPG (`supplier.adgpg.gov.ae`, `adgpg.gov.ae`) is the single entry point; DGE states one-time centralised registration makes the supplier profile visible to buyers across Abu Dhabi Government entities. Suppliers with an Abu Dhabi DED licence self-register; non-Abu Dhabi-licensed and international suppliers must submit an "Entity Request" to the specific entity they want to engage. Some entities (e.g. Abu Dhabi Judicial Department) run their own supplier/tender portals, and some procurement runs through SAP Ariba "Digital Marketplace" with public tenders open to all suppliers. — [Abu Dhabi Media Office](https://www.mediaoffice.abudhabi/en/supplier-registration/); [EAD supplier registration](https://www.ead.gov.ae/en/suppliers/supplier-registration); [rrcdr.gov.ae](https://rrcdr.gov.ae/Supplier-Registration); [ADJD Suppliers Gate](https://www.adjd.gov.ae/en/pages/businessrole.aspx)
- **Dubai:** eSupply is the official procurement portal for Dubai Government tenders, displaying tenders/RFXs from about 40 major Dubai entities with the entire bidding process online; iSupplier is Digital Dubai's tendering system (Smart Supplier app on iOS/Android); DEWA and Dubai Police have their own portals; Department of Economy & Tourism uses the GRP system. Bidders must register on the designated e-procurement system; results are visible to registered suppliers on the platform. — [dubai.ae Government Tendering and Procurement](https://www.dubai.ae/government-procurement); [eSupply](https://esupply.dubai.gov.ae/esupply/web/index.html); [Dubai DET](https://www.dubaidet.gov.ae/en/our-services/for-consumers-and-students/register-as-a-supplier)
- **Legal process stages (federal), in order:** (1) annual procurement plan prepared with the budget, with estimated costs, timelines, evaluation metrics and public-interest objectives (Law Art. 15); (2) registration and acceptance in the supplier registry (Exec. Art. 9) and, for limited tenders, prequalification checking financial/technical/admin capacity (Law Art. 16.4; Exec. Arts. 8, 19); (3) tender announcement on the system with buyer name, method, specs, quantities, terms, evaluation criteria and weights, deadline (Law Arts. 17–18; Exec. Art. 27); (4) bid window **15 business days, extendable by the agency up to 30 business days**, with further extension for clarifications/amendments (Exec. Art. 26; Law Arts. 19, 21); (5) supplier inquiries answered urgently and shared with all bidders without revealing identity; optional pre-bid meeting with recorded notes circulated (Law Art. 20); (6) technical/financial evaluation strictly against published criteria and weights — no undisclosed criteria (Law Art. 22); (7) negotiation mandatory above a value set in the Manual; reverse auctions permitted (Law Art. 23; Exec. Art. 29); (8) exclusion for non-compliance, past failure, unpaid government obligations, missed deadlines, anti-competitive conduct; abnormally low bids may be rejected only after seeking a cost breakdown (Law Arts. 24–25; Exec. Art. 30); (9) award recommendation approved through the Powers Delegation Matrix, winner notified via system (Exec. Art. 31); (10) contract sent to winner within **10 business days** of award notification; winner must return required documents/signed contract/performance bond within **10 business days** (Exec. Art. 31.5–6); (11) award details may be published within **30 days** of award and signature (Law Art. 28; Exec. Art. 32); (12) post-award contract management: amendments require justification, availability of funds and matrix approval; no waiver/subcontracting without prior written approval, with joint liability; performance bond valid through the contract plus **90 days**; payments per contract dates, with sub-supplier terms aligned (Law Arts. 32–35; Exec. Arts. 33, 37–44). — [LEXAI law text](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation full text](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- **Grievance path:** participating supplier may grieve any pre-award decision within **5 business days** of notification, and the award decision within **5 business days** of issue; agency must decide within a period set in the regulation (silence = rejection); the executive regulation establishes a Grievance Committee and procedures. Unsuccessful bidders can ask for written justification, answered within **10 business days**. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 38](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation, Arts. 33, 46–49](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- Contracts and tender documents are drafted in **Arabic**; English may be used where the agency deems necessary, and the contract must specify the governing language. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 39](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165)

### Inferences
- An SME must potentially complete three separate registrations (federal FSR/DPP, ADGPG, Dubai eSupply/iSupplier) plus an ICV certificate to sell across the main UAE public buyers — onboarding friction is material and a strong use case for an assistant.
- Only ~76 open federal opportunities against 5,000+ registered suppliers suggests intense competition per published tender and that a large share of federal buying may occur through catalogue, price agreements, low-value purchases and direct orders rather than open tenders.
- The mandatory 15–30 business-day bid window creates a predictable "prepare in 2–4 weeks" rhythm, while the 10+10 business-day award/contract handshake and 5-day grievance windows are narrow, deadline-sensitive gates an assistant could track.

### Gaps
- No authoritative English-language end-to-end median cycle time (tender publication → award → first payment) was found; the law gives component deadlines only.
- Exact ADGPG/eSupplier step-by-step workflows, required documents and review SLAs were not accessible (portals require login); only the high-level registration model is documented publicly.
- It was not possible to verify from primary sources which federal entities are "active" on DPP beyond the aggregate "30+" figure.

## Key Question 3: What rules or preferences exist for local content, SMEs, offsets, and sustainable procurement?

### Takeaway
The 2024 Executive Regulation converts policy into hard rules: **10% of annual federal procurement spend must go to SMEs**, SMEs get a **10% bonus to their final evaluation score** and a performance-bond exemption, and **National In-Country Value (ICV)** compliance is mandatory and ICV score is an explicit evaluation criterion. Green procurement, Emiratisation and sustainability are mandated "wherever possible" through the Procedures Manual. I found no general civilian "offset" obligation in the UAE federal procurement framework — ICV is the operative local-value mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- **SME set-aside and scoring (Exec. Art. 13):** federal agencies "shall allocate (10%) ten percent of the annual procurement spend to small and medium-sized enterprises"; "an additional (10%) ten percent of the total points shall be applied to the final score in support of SMEs"; SMEs are exempt from performance bonds, with the bond amount instead withheld from initial invoices; agencies must review RFP requirements if an SME argues they unduly limit competition; must develop realistic timelines that encourage SME participation; and may apply economic incentives including reduced/adjusted payment terms or advance payments. — [Executive Regulation, Art. 13](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- MoF's supplier page echoes the SME benefits: **10% bonus to final score**, special SME section/identifier in the Catalogue, **inclusion of at least 3 SMEs in limited tenders**, and performance-bond exemption with retention from initial invoices. — [MoF supplier registration service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- SME definition for federal purposes: a company/sole proprietorship classified as small, medium or micro by the relevant UAE authority (Exec. Art. 1); MoF requires SMEs registered in DPP to be members of the **National Programme for SMEs** at the Ministry of Economy. — [Executive Regulation, Art. 1](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html); [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- The law permits a **margin of preference** for suppliers achieving best public interest/added national value, for locally produced procurements, or to stimulate local SMEs, with details in the implementing regulations (Law Art. 22.2). — [LEXAI law text, Art. 22](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165)
- **ICV (Exec. Art. 14):** federal agencies "shall comply with the requirements of the National In-Country Value (ICV) Program issued by the Cabinet Resolution"; the Procedures Manual sets how bids are evaluated for ICV, how procedures vary by total procurement value, and how exceptions are approved. ICV value is also a named bid-evaluation criterion (Exec. Art. 28.1.e). — [Executive Regulation, Arts. 14 and 28](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- MOIAT describes the National ICV Program as a certificate issued to certified suppliers evaluating their contribution to the local economy, with certified suppliers gaining advantages in tender/contract awards based on ICV score; it was launched under "Projects of the 50" to redirect government and leading national-company procurement spend into the UAE economy. — [MOIAT ICV programme](https://moiat.gov.ae/en/programs/icv); [MOIAT via search result](https://html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=UAE+local+content+ICV+program+government+procurement+preference+Make+it+in+the+Emirates)
- Dubai's ICV programme prioritises firms contributing to Dubai's economy by rewarding local manufacturing, Emiratisation and Emirati ownership; Dubai also has a dedicated **Dubai Green Procurement Policy**. — [Invest in Dubai](https://www.investindubai.gov.ae/en/industries/manufacturing/icv-programme); [dubai.ae](https://www.dubai.ae/government-procurement)
- **Emiratisation (Exec. Art. 15):** agencies must take measures before starting any procurement to encourage potential and registered suppliers to comply with Emiratisation percentages set in the Procedures Manual; the Manual defines exceptions. — [Executive Regulation, Art. 15](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- **Green/sustainable (Law Arts. 2(f), 7(8), 13; Exec. Art. 16):** public interest explicitly includes green companies, sustainable commercial activities and products reducing carbon emissions/environmental damage; agencies must encourage green procurement and sustainable products wherever possible; the Manual sets guidelines and exceptions. The MoF Procedures Guide lists "Sustainability and the Circular Economy" and "Localisation and the National Content Program" as procurement principles. — [LEXAI law text](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation, Art. 16](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html); [MoF Guide page](https://mof.gov.ae/en/public-finance/government-procurement/federal-government-procurement-procedures-guide/)
- Evaluation criteria in Exec. Art. 28 add innovation/creative solutions, supplier and team expertise, capacity, sustainable practices (recyclable materials, reduced environmental impact, renewable energy), and ICV value. — [Executive Regulation, Art. 28](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- Secondary (unverified) sector practice: ICV certification is described as effectively mandatory for tenders with ADNOC, Etisalat/e&, EGA, EDGE Group, Mubadala and AD Ports Group. — [Nour Attorneys](https://www.nourattorneys.com/articles/uae-country-value-icv-compliance); [Velmont Crest](https://velmontcrest.ae/insights/icv-certificate-uae-step-by-step-application/) — *secondary sources, not confirmed by primary buyer policy pages in this research*
- **Conflicting secondary claim:** one low-trust guide says "UAE-registered companies receive a 10% price preference and 10% of contracts are reserved for SMEs". Primary sources say the SME benefit is a **10% score bonus** and **10% of annual spend** (not contracts count), and the "price preference for UAE-registered companies" is not supported by the law/regulation text. — [Low-trust claim](https://insights.epiidosisglobalfin.com/knowledge/uae-government-procurement-tender-guide); contrasted with [Executive Regulation, Art. 13](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)

### Inferences
- buyer behaviour is shaped by three computable scores/gates — SME eligibility, ICV certificate/score, and Emiratisation compliance — so an assistant that tells an SME "which preference applies and what to attach" addresses a real decision point.
- Because ICV weightings and green/Emiratisation rules are delegated to the Arabic Procedures Manual and vary by contract value, suppliers cannot reliably self-calculate their effective score without buyer-side clarification — a concrete information asymmetry an assistant could flag.
- No general offset obligation analogous to defence offsets was found in civilian UAE public procurement; offset-style claims should be treated as sector-specific (defence/energy) rather than a federal procurement rule.

### Gaps
- Exact ICV score weightings by procurement value band, the ICV certificate validity period and approved certifying bodies were not confirmed from a primary source.
- The Cabinet Resolution establishing the National ICV Program (referenced but not numbered in Exec. Art. 14) was not located.
- No primary source was found defining the precise SME qualifying threshold (revenue/headcount) used by the National Programme for SMEs for federal procurement purposes.
- Whether Dubai/Abu Dhabi apply identical 10% SME scoring/spend rules was not verified; emirate-level rules were only confirmed at high level (eSupply/iSupplier/ADGPG portals and Dubai ICV/Green policies).

## Key Question 4: What are the practical pain points and typical cycle times from tender to award and payment?

### Takeaway
Formal federal clocks are comparatively tight (15–30 business-day bids; 10 business days each way for award-to-contract; 5 business-day grievances; award publication within 30 days), but the real-world pain points are severe: multi-year government receivables, non-transparent tendering, no unified national tender platform, and inconsistent federal/emirate rules. No authoritative end-to-end average tender-to-payment time was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Formal clocks:** bid response 15 business days, extendable to 30 (Exec. Art. 26); agency sends contract within 10 business days of award notification, supplier must respond with documents/bond within 10 business days (Exec. Art. 31); unsuccessful-bidder clarification within 10 business days (Exec. Art. 33); award publication within 30 days of award+signature (Law Art. 28; Exec. Art. 32); grievances within 5 business days (Law Art. 38); performance bond remains valid 90 days after contract end (Exec. Art. 1 definitions). — [LEXAI law text](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- **Supplier onboarding clock:** Federal Supplier Register service completion is stated as **30 working days** (no fees), before any tender can be bid. — [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- **Payment and process pain points (high-trust, US Department of Commerce, last published 25 Aug 2025):** "Late payments are an ongoing challenge, especially in public-sector procurements. Companies that have significant experience with government procurement report that they have carried government receivables for years." The same guide lists "Payment delays and issues", "Non-transparent tendering processes", "Lack of a unified government tender online platform", "Lengthy and complicated dispute resolution processes", "Localization and national workforce development requirements", "Inconsistencies and coordination across the seven emirates as well as between federal and emirate-level regulatory bodies", and new laws announced without public consultation. — [trade.gov UAE Market Challenges](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/united-arab-emirates-market-challenges)
- Practice-oriented legal commentary describes UAE government procurement disputes as document-driven, covering tender compliance, bid rejection, award, performance delays, change orders, payment certification, performance bonds, termination and final accounts, with a five-working-day grievance window under Decree-Law 11/2023. — [Hz Legal](https://hzlegal.ae/blog/government-procurement-disputes-in-the-uae-tender-rules-contract-performance-and-payment-claims); [Kayrouz & Associates](https://www.kayrouzandassociates.com/insights/uae-government-tender-disputes-bid-challenges) — *secondary but specialized sources*
- Law-level payment rule: federal agencies must pay suppliers per stipulated provisions and dates; in subcontracting, the main supplier must pay sub-suppliers on agreed timelines aligned with the agency terms — but no specific number of days is stated in the law. — [LEXAI law text, Art. 35](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165)
- MoF offers SMEs "economic incentives… reducing or adjusting payment terms… or approving specific advance payments", implying standard payment terms can be a burden for SMEs. — [Executive Regulation, Art. 13.8](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)
- Scale signal: at the time of research MoF displayed only **76 active opportunities** against **5,000+ active suppliers** and 30+ federal entities — a high bidder-to-tender ratio for published opportunities. — [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/)
- Administrative burden signal: suppliers must supply trade licence, VAT certificate, owner passport (and attested documents for foreign suppliers); procurement documents are Arabic-first; performance bonds (bank guarantees) are required except for SMEs. — [MoF supplier service](https://mof.gov.ae/en/services/register-suppliers-in-the-federal-supplier-register/); [LEXAI law text, Art. 39](https://lexaidxb.com/en/legislations/uae-2165); [Executive Regulation Art. 13.5 and definitions](https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/AE/UAELegislation/finance_and_banking/federal_law/2709.html)

### Inferences
- Statutory SLAs are short and fixed, but they cover only discrete hand-offs; the law deliberately leaves payment dates to the contract, which is where the multi-year receivables reported by US Commerce originate.
- The most consequential gates for an SME assistant are: (1) registration/acceptance (30 working days), (2) ICV certificate and SME programme membership before bidding, (3) bid window (15–30 business days), (4) 10-day document/bond turnaround after award, (5) 5-day grievance deadline, (6) invoice/payment follow-up with no statutory day-count.
- "Non-transparent tendering" and "no unified platform" from trade.gov likely reflect the pre-/early-DPP period; the DPP and ADGPG have since centralised parts of the journey, but fragmentation across emirates and entity-specific portals persists (confirmed by Dubai and Abu Dhabi official pages).
- The combination of high bidder-to-tender ratio, strict published-evaluation rules and non-price scoring (ICV, sustainability, innovation) means "price-only" bidding is a weak strategy; an assistant that helps structure the technical/score response and verify preference eligibility has a clear value proposition.

### Gaps
- No authoritative study gives median or average calendar days from tender publication to award, or from invoice to payment, in the UAE public sector — this is the single most important missing number for cycle-time positioning.
- No reliable 2025/2026 data on the share of UAE federal procurement conducted via catalogue/price agreements vs open tender was found (the 76-active-opportunity figure is a snapshot, not a share).
- No quantified data on SME win rates, rejection reasons or grievance volumes in UAE federal procurement was available in public sources.
- I did not find a recent World Bank/OECD/GCC procurement review specific to UAE 2024–2026 with cycle-time metrics; search results surfaced only general commercial guides and law-firm commentary.
